Framer has taken the web design world by storm. Its fluid, canvas-based editor allows designers, marketers, and developers to build stunning, highly animated, and fully responsive websites without writing a single line of code. However, once you finish designing and decide to point your creation to a custom domain, you are hit with a harsh reality: Framer’s hosting plans.

For hobbyists, side-project builders, indie hackers, and freelancers managing multiple low-traffic sites, Framer’s hosting fees can quickly become a significant financial burden. In this guide, we will analyze the Framer hosting cost problem and explore three practical options to host your Framer website for under $5/month in 2026.

---

## The Framer hosting problem

Framer is an amazing tool for visual design, but its pricing model is heavily anchored on per-site subscription fees. If you want to connect a custom domain (e.g., `yourname.com`), you cannot remain on the free tier. You must upgrade to at least the **Basic** plan, which costs $10 to $15 per month depending on whether you bill monthly or annually. 

For a single professional portfolio or a primary startup landing page, $15/month is reasonable. But the numbers stack up against you when:
1. **You are building side projects:** If you launch 5 small experiments or landing pages to test the market, you will find yourself paying $50 to $75/month just to keep them online.
2. **You have low-traffic pages:** Paying $120 to $180/year for a simple page that receives fewer than 100 visitors a month is hard to justify.
3. **You are a freelancer or agency:** If you maintain low-maintenance staging sites or test variants for clients, hosting overhead eats directly into your profit margins.

Fortunately, you do not have to pay these high fees. Let's look at the alternative ways to run your Framer sites on custom domains for a fraction of the cost.

## Option 1 — Export and host yourself

The most cost-effective way to host your Framer site is to host it as static files. Since Framer compiles your design into standard HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and asset files, you can export these files and host them on free-tier static hosting platforms like Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, or GitHub Pages.

### How to do it
To get your hands on the static code, you have two pathways:
1. **Use Framer's Built-in Export:** If you are already on a paid plan or have an enterprise license, you can export your site as HTML/CSS directly from the Framer project settings.
2. **Use hostfa.st's Exporter:** If you are on Framer's free plan, you can use our free [Framer Site Exporter](http://localhost:3000/tools/framer-export) tool. Simply paste your `.framer.website` preview link, and the exporter will pull all pages, scripts, stylesheets, and assets, bundle them into a single ZIP file, and optimize the links to work relatively.

Once you have your ZIP file:
- Unzip the archive.
- Drag and drop the folder into Vercel or Netlify.
- Connect your custom domain for free.

**Pros:**
- It is 100% free. You only pay for your domain name.
- Incredible global performance via Vercel/Netlify's global networks.

**Cons:**
- **Manual Work:** Every time you make a change in the Framer editor, you must re-export the site, download the ZIP, and re-upload it to Vercel or Netlify. This becomes frustrating if you update your site frequently.

## Option 3 — Switch to another builder

If you are open to using tools other than Framer, you could switch to alternative website builders. Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, and WordPress are all common alternatives.

However, most of these builders charge similar or even higher monthly hosting fees. For example:
- **Webflow** starts at $15/month for a custom domain.
- **Squarespace** starts at $16/month.
- **Wix** starts at $16/month.

If you choose this route, you not only lose Framer's unmatched design-to-canvas velocity, but you will also end up paying equivalent hosting costs. Therefore, switching builders to save on hosting is rarely a good trade-off.
